# Why We Forget

In 1885 Hermann Ebbinghaus published the results of an unusual experiment: he memorized lists of nonsense syllables and tested himself at intervals to see how much he had lost.

The result is now called the forgetting curve. Most forgetting happens in the first hours and days, then the curve flattens. A large share of new material is gone within a day unless it is reviewed.

Two practical lessons follow:
- Review early. The first review should come soon after learning, when forgetting is fastest.
- Each review flattens the curve, so the next review can wait longer. This is the reasoning behind spaced repetition.

A caveat: Ebbinghaus studied meaningless syllables on himself. Meaningful material that connects to what you already know is forgotten more slowly, which is one reason linked notes help.
