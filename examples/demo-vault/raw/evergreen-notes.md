# Evergreen Notes

Andy Matuschak, a researcher who writes about tools for thought, uses the term "evergreen notes" for notes written to evolve and accumulate value over time, as opposed to transient notes taken once and never read again.

His principles overlap with the Zettelkasten:
- Notes should be atomic: about one thing.
- Notes should be concept-oriented, not organized by source or book.
- Notes should be densely linked.
- Prefer associative structure over rigid folders.

The point is that writing notes this way is itself a form of thinking. A note you have to phrase precisely, in your own words, forces you to understand the idea.

That makes evergreen notes a cousin of the Feynman Technique: if you cannot write the note simply, you do not understand it yet. And rewriting an idea from memory, instead of copying it, is active recall. A good note-taking habit doubles as a study habit, and a spaced repetition deck works better when its cards come from notes you already understand.
