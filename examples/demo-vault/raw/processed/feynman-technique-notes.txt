Notes on the Feynman Technique

The method, named after physicist Richard Feynman:
1. Pick a concept.
2. Explain it in plain language as if teaching someone new to the subject.
3. Wherever you stall or reach for jargon, that is a gap. Go back to the source.
4. Simplify and use an analogy.

Why it works: explaining forces retrieval and exposes the illusion of understanding you get from rereading.

Note to self: Feynman never wrote this down as a "technique". The name and the four steps were packaged later by other people, based on how he described learning and teaching.
