# Reading Notes: Deep Work

Cal Newport, a computer science professor at Georgetown, argues in Deep Work (2016) that the ability to focus without distraction on cognitively demanding tasks is becoming rare and valuable at the same time.

Key ideas:
- Deep work produces new value and improves your skills. Shallow work (email, meetings, logistics) is easy to replicate.
- Switching tasks leaves "attention residue": part of your mind stays on the previous task. The term comes from organizational psychologist Sophie Leroy.
- Schedule deep work in blocks and protect them, rather than waiting for free time to appear.

My take: this pairs with note-taking. If I capture ideas quickly during deep work and process them later, I keep the focus block clean.
